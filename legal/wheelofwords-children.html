Children's Privacy Notice

Wheel of Words · For parents and guardians · Last updated: 12 August 2026

This notice is for the adult responsible for a child playing Wheel of Words. It is deliberately short and avoids legal wording. It sits alongside our full Privacy Policy, which covers every age group.

The short version

When a child under 13 plays, we collect nothing about them. There is no account, nothing is uploaded, and nothing your child does in the game reaches us. The one exception is described below, and it contains no information about your child.

How the app knows it is a child

The first time the app opens it asks for a date of birth, with no default answer and no suggestion of which answer is better. If the date given is under 13, the app runs in Kids Mode from then on.

The date of birth never leaves the device. It is not sent to us, and we do not store it. It is used once, on the device, to decide which version of the app to build.

What Kids Mode does not do

These are not settings switched off, and they are not features hidden behind a check. The software that would do these things is never started in a child's session, so there is nothing that could be turned back on by accident.

  • No account and no sign-in — not even an anonymous one.
  • No cloud backup. Progress never leaves the device.
  • No analytics of any kind.
  • No advertising. There is no advertising software in a child's session at all.
  • No purchases, and nothing to buy anywhere in the app.
  • No notifications or reminders.
  • No leaderboard, no usernames, no profile picture, and no way to be seen by other players.
  • No sharing, and no links out to the web or to other apps.

The one thing that is sent

If the app crashes, it sends us a crash report. We do this in every mode, including Kids Mode, because a crash in a child's game is still a bug worth fixing.

A crash report describes the failure, not the player: the device model, the operating system version, and where in our code the error happened. No identifier for your child is attached — the app never sends one in Kids Mode — and none of your child's progress, words or settings is included.

What is stored on the device

Levels completed, stars, the words your child has met and how well they know them, and the app's own settings. All of it stays on the device. Deleting the app removes all of it, permanently. There is nothing on our servers to ask us to delete, because nothing was ever sent.

What your child sees

Kids Mode uses a smaller, separately checked set of words and only the first two worlds of the game. Every word a child can be shown has been screened for age-appropriateness, and a level is only offered in Kids Mode if every word in it passed that check.

The parental gate

The few places that lead outside the game — settings, and changing the mode the app runs in — are behind a simple arithmetic question that a young child is unlikely to answer. This is the pattern Google Play recommends. It is a speed bump, not a security measure, and it is not a substitute for knowing what your child is playing.

If you think we have data about your child

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13. If a child has used a version of the app that was not in Kids Mode — for example, if an older person's date of birth was entered — then an account may exist.

Email us and we will delete that account and everything in it within 7 days, and switch the app to Kids Mode. You do not need to prove anything or fill in a form; tell us and we will act on it.

Your rights

As the parent or guardian you may ask us what we hold about your child, ask us to delete it, and refuse any further collection. In Kids Mode the honest answer to the first question is "nothing", and we would rather tell you that than send you a form.
