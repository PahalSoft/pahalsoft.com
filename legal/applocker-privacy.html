Privacy Policy

App Locker · Last updated: 21 August 2026

App Locker is made by PahalSoft (“we”, “our”, “us”). This policy explains what the app stores, what it does not, and why you do not have to take either claim on trust.

1. The short version

App Locker collects nothing and sends nothing. That is not a statement of intent — the app does not hold Android's INTERNET permission, so it has no technical ability to open a network connection at all. You can confirm this from your own device's settings before you ever trust us on it. See section 9.

2. What is stored on your device

All of the following stays in App Locker's private storage, which other apps cannot read:

  • Which apps you chose to protect — so it knows what to lock.
  • A cryptographic hash of your PIN — so it can check your PIN without storing it.
  • Your settings — auto-lock timing, whether biometric unlock is on.
  • A count of recent failed attempts — to slow down repeated guessing.

Your PIN itself is never stored. What is kept is a hash produced using a key held in the Android Keystore, from which the PIN cannot be recovered. If you forget your PIN, nobody — including us — can retrieve it for you.

3. What is not stored, anywhere

  • No record of which apps you opened, or when.
  • No app content, messages, passwords or screenshots.
  • No accessibility text from any app.
  • No device identifiers, advertising ID or location.
  • No name, email address or account of any kind.
  • No crash reports and no analytics.

There is no account to create, and no server for it to be created on.

4. The Accessibility Service

App Locker uses Android's Accessibility Service for exactly one purpose: to notice when an app comes to the foreground, so that a lock screen can be shown if it is one you chose to protect.

The service reads only the package name of the app being opened — an identifier such as com.example.messaging. It does not read screen contents, text, notifications, or anything you type. That is a limit in how the app uses the API, not a policy you are asked to trust.

Android shows a strongly worded warning when you enable any accessibility service, because the API can be used to read screen content. App Locker does not use it that way — and because it has no internet permission, even data it never collects would have nowhere to go.

5. Biometric unlock

If you turn on fingerprint or face unlock, the check is performed by Android itself. App Locker receives only a yes-or-no answer. Your biometric data never reaches the app, because Android does not expose it to apps at all.

6. Data sharing

None. There is nobody to share with: no analytics provider, no advertising network, no cloud storage, no crash reporting service, and no third-party component that transmits anything.

7. Diagnostics

App Locker can produce a short diagnostic summary that you may choose to send us when asking for help. It contains only device and service status — Android version, whether the accessibility service is running, and timing measurements. It contains no app names and no personal data, and it is never sent anywhere unless you deliberately share it yourself.

8. Children

App Locker is not directed at children, and collects no data from anyone — including children.

9. How to verify all of this

You do not have to take our word for any of it:

  • Check the permissions. Android Settings → Apps → App Locker → Permissions. The only permission listed is biometric access. No internet, no storage, no contacts, no camera.
  • Check the network usage. Settings → Network & internet → Data usage. App Locker will not appear, because it cannot make a connection.

An app without the internet permission cannot open a network connection. Android enforces that at the system level — it is not something the app chooses to honour.

10. Deleting your data

Uninstalling App Locker removes everything it stored. There is no residue, because there was never a copy anywhere else. You can also clear it at any time from Android Settings → Apps → App Locker → Storage.

11. What App Locker does not protect against

This belongs in a privacy policy because overstating it would be the dishonest part. App Locker deters someone casually picking up your unlocked phone. It is not protection against someone with time, technical skill and physical access to your device: anyone who can reach Android's own settings can disable App Locker, and anyone who can uninstall apps can remove it. It does not encrypt your files, and it is not a replacement for your device's lock screen.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, it will be because the app changed. Our rule is that the statements above must remain literally true — a change that would make any of them false does not ship. Updates are posted on this page with a new date at the top.
