Privacy Policy

Spruce · Last updated: 27 November 2026

Spruce is made by PahalSoft (“we”, “our”, “us”). It is a desktop app that looks after an Ubuntu computer, which means it reads a lot about that computer. This policy explains what it reads, what it keeps, where anything goes — and that none of it comes to us.

1. The short version

Spruce sends nothing to PahalSoft. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no server of ours for anything to go to. That includes Spruce Pro: your licence is checked on your own computer, and Spruce never contacts us to see whether you paid. What Spruce learns about your computer is shown to you, on your computer, and that is the end of it.

2. What Spruce reads on your computer

To do its job, Spruce reads, only when you use the matching feature:

  • File and folder sizes — for Cleanup, Space Map and My Files.
  • File contents, to compare them — Duplicates compares file contents, and Similar Photos makes a small fingerprint of each picture. Neither leaves your computer.
  • Installed apps and available updates — from Ubuntu’s package system, Snap and Flatpak.
  • Running programs, memory and processor use — for Performance.
  • Security settings — such as whether the firewall is on — for Protection.
  • File names and sizes in your cloud storage — only if you open the Cloud page, and only through accounts you connected yourself.

3. What Spruce stores, and where

Everything stays in your own home folder:

  • A history of cleanups — what was cleaned and how much space it freed — in ~/.local/state/spruce/.
  • The result of the last weekly check, if you turned the weekly check on, in the same folder.
  • Photo fingerprints, so that later Similar Photos scans are quick, in ~/.cache/spruce/.
  • Quarantined files, if the virus scanner found something and you chose to quarantine it, in ~/.local/share/spruce/quarantine/, locked so they cannot be opened.
  • Your Spruce Pro licence, if you entered one, in ~/.config/spruce/licence.key, readable only by you.
  • The date you started the Spruce Pro trial, if you did, in ~/.config/spruce/trial.json.

4. Connections Spruce causes — none of them to us

Spruce itself does not connect to the internet. Some features do ask Ubuntu’s own tools to do something that involves a connection, exactly as if you had used those tools yourself:

  • Updates — Ubuntu’s package system, Snap, Flatpak and the firmware updater contact their usual update servers (for example Ubuntu’s archive, the Snap Store, Flathub and the Linux Vendor Firmware Service).
  • Virus definitions — if you install ClamAV, its own updater downloads virus definitions from ClamAV’s servers.
  • Cloud — the Cloud page reads file lists from Google Drive or OneDrive through the accounts you connected in Ubuntu’s Online Accounts, or through rclone if you set it up.

Those services have their own privacy policies. Spruce adds nothing to these connections and receives nothing from them that is sent on to anyone.

5. Administrator access

Some tasks need administrator rights, so Spruce asks for your password through Ubuntu’s standard password dialog. Your password goes to Ubuntu, not to Spruce: Spruce never sees it and never stores it. What then runs with those rights is a separate helper that can only perform a fixed list of named actions.

6. Buying Spruce Pro

When you buy Spruce Pro, you write to us by email and we reply with how to pay. We keep your name, your email address and the details of your purchase, because we need them to send your licence, to re-send it if you lose it, and for our accounts and tax records. We do not use them for anything else, and we do not share them except where the law requires it or with the payment service you choose to pay through, which has its own privacy policy.

Your licence contains your name, your email address, a licence number and its dates. It is signed so that Spruce can tell it is genuine; checking it happens entirely on your computer.

7. Data sharing

The app shares nothing: there is no analytics provider, no advertising network, no cloud storage of ours and no crash reporting service. The only information we hold about you is what you send us when you buy Spruce Pro, as described above.

8. Children

Spruce is not directed at children, and collects no data from anyone — including children.

9. How to verify this

Spruce is free software under the GNU GPL, so its behaviour can be checked rather than taken on trust. You can also watch it yourself: everything it stores is in the folders listed in section 3, and a network monitor will show that the app makes no connections of its own.

10. Deleting your data

Uninstalling Spruce removes the app. To remove what it stored, delete these folders from your home folder: ~/.local/state/spruce, ~/.cache/spruce and ~/.local/share/spruce and ~/.config/spruce. Check the quarantine folder first if you might want to restore anything from it. Spruce keeps no copy anywhere else. If you bought Spruce Pro and want us to delete your purchase details, write to us; we will, except for what we must keep by law.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, it will be because the app changed. The statements above must remain literally true — a change that would make any of them false does not ship. Updates are posted on this page with a new date at the top.
