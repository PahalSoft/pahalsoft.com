Light and colour

Sixteen adjustments from exposure and contrast to texture and dehaze, with tone curves and per-colour hue and saturation when you want to be precise.

Crop that stays honest

Straighten and rotate with the frame pulled in automatically, so a levelled horizon never leaves an empty corner in the exported file.

Nothing is final

Every edit is a step you can walk back. Reopen a photo weeks later and the whole history is still there, because none of it was ever applied to the original.

On-device is not a setting. It is where the work happens.

Plenty of editors send your picture to a server, do the work there, and send a result back. Timisha does the work on your phone's own graphics hardware. There is no upload step to opt out of, because there is no upload.

About permissions

Timisha asks for no access to your photo library. You pick a photo with Android's own picker and the app receives that one file. It does hold the internet permission, and we would rather say so here than have you find it in the listing and wonder — it arrives with Google's billing library, which is how a purchase is verified.

Your photographs are not part of that. They are decoded, edited and exported entirely on the device, and no part of the app sends image data anywhere. If a future version ever needed to, it would say so before doing it — not in a policy update after the fact.

Free, and what Pro adds.

The everyday work is free and stays free: all sixteen adjustments, crop and straighten, auto-tone, the built-in looks, unlimited undo, and export at full resolution with no watermark. An editor that cannot give you your photo back is not an editor.

Pro is one purchase, not a subscription. It removes the ceilings — unlimited masks instead of two per photo, selection by colour and tone, per-channel curves, per-colour adjustment, sharpening and noise reduction, and the other export formats.

What it is good at — and what it is not

Timisha is built for finishing a photograph you already have: correcting light and colour, working on one part of the frame without disturbing the rest, and getting a full-quality file back out. It is good at that.

It is not a compositor and it does not generate imagery. It will not remove an object, replace a sky with one that was never there, or invent detail that the sensor did not record. Automatic subject selection is a starting point you then refine by hand, not a promise that the machine always gets it right — and when it does not, every parameter behind it is yours to adjust.
